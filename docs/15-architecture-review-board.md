# 15 — Enterprise Architecture Review Board: Findings & Scores

> Adversarial review of blueprint Docs 00–14 by a combined CTO / EA / Quality / Ops-Excellence / AI / Security / Product-Strategy board.
> Mandate: break the architecture, not praise it. Target bar: 1,000,000 properties across Pakistan with 10-year maintainability.
> Verdict up front: **the blueprint is strong on operating philosophy and lifecycle rigor, but it is NOT yet coding-ready.** It has one unpriced business risk (deal leakage), one structural incentive conflict (Area Manager approves what they're paid on), a Phase-1 dependency on manual logging discipline that will not survive contact with reality, and a set of missing Pakistan-specific compliance and master-data items. 12 findings are P0 (fix before any code).

Severity: **CRIT** (kills the business or the trust asset) · **HIGH** (material damage/cost) · **MED** (friction, drift, rework) · **LOW** (polish).
Priority: **P0** = fix in blueprint before coding · **P1** = design before its module ships · **P2** = revisit at named phase gate.

# SECOND-WAVE REVIEW — Stress-testing the "One Million Properties" Claim

> The first wave found module-level defects. The second wave asks a harder question the board is required to answer: *does this architecture actually survive 1,000,000 verified properties across Pakistan?* On re-read, the answer is **no — not as written** — for three reasons that are more serious than anything in Wave 1. The headline finding (SW-1) is arguably the single most important sentence in this entire review.

## 5. Wave-2 Findings

### The Scale Arithmetic Nobody Did

| ID | Sev | Finding | Why → consequence | Fix | Pri | Pre-code? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SW-1** | **CRIT** | **The verification model does not arithmetically scale to 1M properties.** Badge validity is 90 days (Doc 05). 1M verified properties therefore require **~1M re-verifications every quarter = ~11,000 renewals PER DAY nationally**, before a single *new* property is added. At 8 field-verifications/agent/day that is **~1,400 field agents running purely to stand still** on renewals — a payroll of a scale the commission economics almost certainly cannot carry. The blueprint's own unit economics (Doc 01 §2.2) price the *initial* visit and never price the renewal treadmill. This is the difference between a business and a bonfire. | The trust asset (dated verification) is also the cost bomb. Freshness — the differentiator — is the thing that makes the model collapse under its own weight at scale. Growth in verified supply *increases* the fixed renewal cost linearly and forever. | **Tiered re-verification, designed now:** (1) full field re-verification only on trigger (complaint, risk score, price change, long vacancy) or annually, not quarterly; (2) **cheap renewal tier** — owner-initiated guided re-confirmation via time-sealed self-capture link (a photo of the meter + a liveness selfie through the owner portal), agent drive-by only for anomalies; (3) **remote video verification** for renewals; (4) trust status shows *method* (field-verified vs owner-confirmed vs remote) so cheaper tiers don't dilute the premium badge. Model the renewal cost curve explicitly in the A-2 budget module. Until this exists, "1M properties" is not a supportable target. | P0 | **Yes — this reshapes verification, KPIs, and the entire cost model** |
| **SW-2** | **CRIT** | **No supply/demand balance governor.** The architecture is supply-obsessed (verify, verify, verify) but the throughput to actually *rent* 1M properties — matching, visits, closings — is never capacity-sized. 1M verified listings with insufficient matched demand = mass staleness = the exact failure the company exists to kill, at 1M-scale. Verifying supply nobody rents is the most expensive way to fail. | Areas fill with verified-but-unrented listings; freshness costs (SW-1) pile onto inventory generating no commission; the North-Star (closings) decouples from the vanity metric (verified count) and the company optimizes the wrong one. | Introduce a **supply/demand balance metric per area** as a first-class governor: target verified-live inventory as a *function of measured demand* (leads, searches, closings), not a raw maximum. The planner should throttle verification target generation in areas where inventory-to-closing ratio exceeds a band. Verification is demand-pulled, not supply-pushed. Add to Doc 07 target cascade and Doc 11 dashboards. | P0 | **Yes — target-generation logic** |
| **SW-3** | HIGH | **Single primary write-node is a hard ceiling at the blueprint's own NFR-4 numbers** (5M properties, 50M leads, 100M tasks, 500M audit events). Partitioning + read replicas (Doc 04) scale *reads*; they do not scale *writes*. There is no sharding strategy, no write-scaling trigger points, no CQRS boundary for the event-heavy path. | Year 3–4 hits a write ceiling with no rehearsed escape; emergency re-sharding of a live national system is the kind of quarter that kills companies. | A **data-scaling roadmap with trigger metrics**: (1) hot tables (tasks, audit, events, interactions, notifications) move to a separate write-optimized/append store early; (2) geographic sharding by city as the natural shard key (aligns with the org/area scope model — cheap because scoping already exists); (3) named thresholds ("at X writes/sec or Y GB, execute shard plan Z"). Decide the shard key *now* while migration is trivial. | P0 | **Yes — shard key is a schema-shaping decision** |
| **SW-4** | HIGH | **The event outbox is a single logical spine with no backpressure, dead-letter, per-consumer lag SLO, or poison-message handling** (Doc 04 §1.1). One slow/broken consumer (search, warehouse, AI, notifications) causes unbounded outbox growth and cross-consumer contagion. | At 500M events, one wedged consumer degrades the whole platform; replays double-count KPIs → payroll disputes (F-6/E-3 territory) at national scale. | Formalize event infrastructure: partitioned log with independent consumer offsets, per-consumer lag SLOs + alerts, dead-letter queues, poison-message quarantine, and **idempotency keys on every money- and message-emitting consumer** (exactly-once for incentives and WhatsApp sends; at-least-once elsewhere with dedup). This is the Uber/Airbnb-scale lesson the blueprint hasn't absorbed. | P0 | **Yes — event contract + idempotency discipline** |
| **SW-5** | HIGH | **The universal Task object is a God-object at scale.** Wave 1 praised "everything is a task"; the board now flags the flip side: at 100M rows it is simultaneously the hottest table, the widest polymorphic schema, the tightest coupling point, and the maximum change-amplification center in the system. Every module writes it; every planner run scans it. | The single component most likely to become the scaling and maintainability bottleneck is the one the architecture leans on hardest. | Keep the *logical* universal task contract (it's a genuine strength) but **physically partition tasks by domain** (verification-tasks, comms-tasks, marketing-tasks…) behind a shared interface + shared SLA/evidence services, time-partitioned, with completed tasks aging to cold storage.
